summary of experiments (see nonsym.log, orthomin.log and precond.log for detail)
================================================================

a. spd systems
   the search rediscovers conjugate gradient exactly:
     beta_k  = (r.r) / (r.r)_prev      (fletcher reeves)
     alpha_k = (r.r) / (d.ad)          (cg step length)
   on the five held out spd problems it ties cg iteration for iteration
   (129, 51, 18, 120, 207). see results/summary.txt and convergence.png.

b. nonsymmetric systems, single stored direction (5 seeds, held out test set,
   cost = matrix vector products because bicgstab does two per iteration)
   best discovered short recurrence solver:
     beta_k  = 0.5632 * (r.p) / (r.r)_prev
     alpha_k = 1.15   * (d.ad) / (ad.ad)
   findings: beats orthomin(1), the fair same memory baseline, on ~65% of held
   out cases, but does not beat gmres or bicgstab in general and generalises
   poorly to the unseen recirculating family. a real but modest result.

c. nonsymmetric systems, multi direction (gcr / orthomin-m)  [the headline]
   giving the search a window of m previous directions, still one matvec per
   step, it rediscovers gcr:
     beta_j = -(a r . a p_j) / (a p_j . a p_j)      alpha = (r . a d) / (a d . a d)
   this fixes the generalisation gap (recirc 334 -> 30 matvecs), beats bicgstab
   on some families at half the matvec cost, and ties gmres(30) while storing
   only a handful of vectors. competitive with the state of the art, recovering a
   known method from scratch.

d. polynomial preconditioner discovery (solver fixed to cg)
   discovered degree 3 coefficients (approx): +1.7, +1.7, +0.66, +0.07
   cuts cg iteration count by roughly half on every test problem, for example
   poisson 44 -> 21, cond1e4 169 -> 105, diagdom 18 -> 7. the honest tradeoff:
   the degree 3 polynomial applies a three extra times per step, so it costs
   more serial matrix vector products. it wins on iterations, the cost that
   dominates on parallel and communication bound hardware.
